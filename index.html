About me

I'm an AI engineer who cares less about models and more about the people who have to trust them. Over six years I've moved from data pipelines to analytics to building AI agents, always for the person on the other end who doesn't work in tech and can't afford a wrong answer.

I've done that inside a city government, a public-media newsroom, and a global consultancy, across three countries. I write about AI governance and social good at aitalks4good, help spotlight women in tech, and I'm happiest turning a messy human problem into software that quietly works.

Musings

Half-formed thoughts on AI, life, and where humans are heading, that I keep coming back to.

Everyone's asking what AI can do. I care more about how we let it into society: who it answers to, who it leaves out, and whether the people it touches ever got a say.

I've built in both the private and public sector. Private runs on profit, and it will pay almost any price to get there. Public runs on ethics and principle, yet the tools arrive late, not because the work is risky, but because accountability is thin and the will is missing. It was never really about the AI. So how do we find the middle ground?

Why is everything black or white, and nothing allowed to be neutral? AI is cast as saviour or threat, good or bad, but most real things live in the grey. Maybe the harder, more honest work is refusing to pick a side.
